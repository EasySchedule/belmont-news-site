The Barnesville B&O Tunnel and Trail is open to the public, and the list of work the village is not satisfied with is still being written, Village Administrator Roger Deal told the Village Council on September 21. Deal reported that the project is "finally complete." In the same report he said: "There'll still be a walk through and punch list to be developed for anything that is not to our satisfaction, but the tunnel and trail are open for use," Barnesville News wrote, the outlet that reported the meeting. Deal set no date for the walk-through or the list. The Barnesville News report of that meeting does not say the tunnel is unsafe, and the village has posted no minutes.

Council President Brad English presided over the September 21 meeting. Mayor Jake Hershberger and council member Mark Lucas were absent, Barnesville News reported.

Barnesville Police Chief Matthew Tippie told council his preparations for Pumpkin Festival crowds were in place. "I have all preparations in place. We're going to be out there as much as possible and are going to try out best to keep everybody safe," Tippie said, Barnesville News reported, which described the remark as concerning the festival. The village's own records show council confirmed Tippie's appointment by Resolution 4055 on July 27.

Belmont News could not confirm when the Pumpkin Festival is being held. Barnesville News reported in August that the festival runs "Friday through Sunday, September 25-27," and in a September 24 article that the depot committee would open the station, caboose and cigar museum "Friday through Sunday." In its October 4 report on the September 21 council meeting, the same outlet quoted Barnesville Depot Committee member Bruce Yarnall as saying the group "is operating the 'Little Roaming Railroad' through the tunnel this coming weekend" as part of the group's Pumpkin Festival activities. October 4 was a Sunday. Belmont News could not reconcile those accounts and has not confirmed festival dates. Yarnall writes the Barnesville News articles quoted here, and discloses in his own coverage that he is a member of the depot committee.

In the same September 21 meeting, council unanimously suspended the village's bidding rules to hire Shelly and Sands to repave South Broadway Street, Barnesville News reported. The vote followed approval of Resolution 4057 as an emergency measure, to pave South Broadway Street as soon as current underground utility work is complete. Project contractor Adam Ackerman told council that asphalt operations are wrapping up for the season, and that a six-week ordinance process with three readings would push the decision past fall plant closings.

The village's own record of the resolution names the statute it relies on. The Village of Barnesville lists Resolution 4057, approved September 21, as "A Resolution Authorizing The Village Administrator To Enter Into A Contract As A Result Of An Emergency Pursuant To Section 735.051 Of The Ohio Revised Code To Permit A Contract To Complete The South Broadway Street Project In The Village Of Barnesville Without Public Bidding Of The Project And Declaring An Emergency."

Section 735.051 of the Ohio Revised Code is titled "Emergency conditions obviate formal bidding and advertising for contracts." The section provides that, in the case of "a real and present emergency," the legislative authority of a municipality may "by a two-thirds vote of all the members elected thereto" authorize the contract without the formal bidding and advertising. Barnesville News reported the vote to waive bidding was unanimous. Belmont News has not reviewed whether Resolution 4057 complies with the statute, and this article does not say that it does or does not.

Barnesville News also reported: "Normally, expenditures over $77,000 require a bidding process." Belmont News could not confirm that figure. The Ohio Department of Commerce set the competitive bidding threshold for January 1 through December 31, 2026 at $79,568, under Section 9.17 of the Revised Code. Section 9.17 sets the amount at $75,000 through calendar year 2024 and then indexes it 3 percent a year, as determined and published by the director of commerce. Section 731.141, which governs what a village administrator may contract for, points to that Section 9.17 amount. Section 731.14, which covers contracts made by a village council, points to the same amount but by its own terms does not apply to a village that has appointed a village administrator under Section 735.271, which Barnesville has. The Barnesville News figure does not match the $79,568 the department set for 2026. Barnesville's own bidding ordinance was not reachable at press time.

Belmont News could not obtain the expected cost of the South Broadway work, so it cannot say whether the project would have required competitive bidding. The village posts Resolution 4057 only as a scanned image with no readable text, and the minutes of the September 21 meeting are not yet posted on the village's website.

One project did go out to bid. Deal told council that bidding on the North Waterline Project to Hendrysburg and Fairview is expected to launch in the coming month, with three weeks of published notices, a mandatory pre-bid meeting in the municipal building and a bid opening a week later, Barnesville News reported.

Council meets tonight. The Village of Barnesville posted a notice that council will meet Monday, October 5, at 7 p.m. in Council Chambers at the Barnesville Municipal Building, 132 N. Arch Street, second floor. The posted agenda is a scanned image, and Belmont News could not read its contents.
