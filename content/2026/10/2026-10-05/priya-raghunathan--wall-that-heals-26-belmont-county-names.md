## Body

The Wall That Heals came to the Belmont County Fairgrounds on a Tuesday and left on a Sunday,
and in between it stood at 45420 Roscoe Road in St. Clairsville like a piece of Washington, D.C.
that had been driven inland.

It was open 24 hours a day, and admission was free. Those two details are on the organizer's
own event site, `wallthathealsohio2026.com`, and on the Belmont County Tourism Council's
event page, `visitbelmontcounty.com`.

The wall is a traveling replica of the Vietnam Veterans Memorial in Washington. Per the
organizer's site, it is a three-quarter-scale copy and it "lists the 58,281 names on the
original Wall so communities can visit close to home." The exhibit was presented by American
Legion St. Clairsville Post 159.

The county's own record says how many of those names were local.

At the Belmont County Board of Commissioners' regular meeting on Sept. 16, Jackee Pugh,
executive director of the Belmont County Tourism Council, gave the board a fall tourism
update and said the Wall That Heals event would run Oct. 1-4, 2026. Her update is in the
signed minutes the board posts online: "There are more than 58,000 service members' names on
the wall, 26 of those names are from Belmont County."

Twenty-six. That is the figure that comes from a Belmont County government record, and it is
not in any of the event's promotional material. It is the number a county reader should hold
onto.

### Why the ceremony was called "Welcome Home"

The Thursday opening ceremony at noon was titled "Welcome Home," and Belmont County Tourism's
event page explains what that name was meant to do. The ceremony, that page says, "is intended
to give Vietnam Veterans a proper return welcome, which many did not receive."

The organizer's site frames the exhibit the same way from the other side. It says the wall
honors the more than three million Americans who served in the Vietnam War, "including the
58,281 service members who never returned home."

So the exhibit that stood at the fairgrounds for four days was built around a homecoming
that, for most of the people it honored, had never happened.

Other events on the schedule, all on the organizer's site: a volunteer orientation at 6 p.m.
on Sept. 30, the day before opening; "Playing of Taps" at dusk on Oct. 1 and again on Oct. 3;
and "Light the Night" at 7:45 p.m. Friday, Oct. 2. Sunday's closing ceremony was set for 1:45
p.m., and the organizer's site says the exhibit closed at 2 p.m. when the ceremony ended.

The wall arrived by escort procession on Sept. 29, according to the organizer's schedule. The
organizer's site describes that day as an arrival "with a special escort procession beginning in
Wheeling, WV," with the convoy leaving the Bass Pro Shop at 1 Cabela Dr, Triadelphia, W.Va., at
noon and reaching the fairgrounds at 1 p.m. Set-up began at 8:30 a.m. on Sept. 30.

### What the organizers built for the county

The Wall That Heals includes a mobile Education Center that travels with the replica, and
the organizer used it for something specific to this stop. During the Belmont County visit,
service members whose names appear on the wall and who listed communities near Belmont County
as their home of record were to be recognized as "Hometown Heroes" inside the Education
Center, the organizer's site says. That display was meant to make the point that the names
on the wall were not distant figures, but people who lived here.

American Legion Post 159 was the presenting sponsor, according to the organizer's site, with
Belmont County itself, the Belmont County Fair, Belmont County Tourism and Rotary of
St. Clairsville listed as "Freedom Sponsors." Belmont County Tourism's page says St. Clairsville
was "one of just 31 communities nationwide selected to host this powerful traveling replica."

### Who spoke at the close

The Times Leader reported that Belmont County Commissioner Vince Gianangeli spoke at Sunday's
closing ceremony, along with Wall That Heals site manager Tim Tetz and music by JoAnn
Copeland. Belmont News could not confirm the appearance from the county's own website: the
commissioners' News & Events page carries no events, and the signed board minutes published
on the county site run only through Sept. 16, 2026, the week before the exhibit opened. The
commissioners' own biography page confirms Gianangeli is vice president of the three-member
board and is serving his first term.

The Times Leader also reported the closing-ceremony remarks. It is the only outlet Belmont
News found reporting the closing, and those remarks are its reporting, not ours. It quoted
Gianangeli thanking the crowd: "Thank you, veterans. Thank you, families for trusting this
community with your stories, your memories and in many cases your grief," he said.

### The number that does not agree

Here is the problem with the most repeated figure from this event.

Two pages a reader can open say the replica carries 58,281 names:

- the organizer's event site, `wallthathealsohio2026.com`, which prints "58,281" twice, in
  its opening description and again in its "About The Wall That Heals" section;
- the Belmont County Tourism Council event page, which says the exhibit "bears the names of
  the 58,281 men and women who made the ultimate sacrifice."

The Times Leader's story on the closing ceremony says the wall "has 58,318 names on it."

Those are different numbers, and Belmont News could not reconcile them from any page we could
open. Belmont County's own minutes do not settle it either; they round, saying "more than
58,000." We are not going to average two numbers, and we are not going to quietly print the
one that appears in the paper on the stand.

So the checkable version is this: **58,281 names, on the organizer's site and the county
tourism page. 58,318 names, in the Times Leader.** Belmont News has not established which is
correct, and readers who see either number can check where it came from.

### How many people came

Tim Tetz, the Wall That Heals site manager, told the Times Leader that more than 8,000 people
came during the visit, and at the end of the week said "we had over 8,000 visitors."
Belmont News was unable to reach Tetz for this story, so that figure is the Times Leader's
report of his remarks, attributed to him by name.

The Times Leader also quoted Tetz on why turnout was strong here, comparing this part of the
country to West Virginia's per-capita losses on the wall, and singled out Beallsville, Pa. And
it quoted Larry Barnes, a Vietnam veteran and commander of American Legion Post 159, who said:
"I think our turnout was more than we expected. Their first time going to the wall, the
veterans, they had a lot of memories to share. Finding their buddies on the wall, saying their
final goodbye to their buddies. It was a very healing process."

That reporting is the Times Leader's. We checked the county record and the organizer's site
for a local count of visitors and found none published.

### What is left

The wall is gone. The fairgrounds has its calendar back.

What Belmont County has that it did not have on Sept. 29 is a set of 26 names that the county
itself, in a signed public record, identifies as belonging to its own residents, and a
four-day record of people standing at that wall in the dark looking for someone they loved.

## Editor's note — out-of-area references

Three things named above are outside Belmont County and are included for stated reasons.

- **Wheeling and Triadelphia, W.Va.** — the wall's arrival route. The organizer's Sept. 29
  schedule says the wall arrived "with a special escort procession beginning in Wheeling, WV,"
  and gives the convoy's departure point as the Bass Pro Shop, 1 Cabela Dr, Triadelphia, W.Va.
  This is in the story because the escort came through; it is not a Belmont County stop. The
  schedule gives no mileage from either town, so this story states none.
- **West Virginia and Beallsville, Pa.** — both appear only inside Tim Tetz's quoted remarks,
  as he compares Ohio Valley losses to West Virginia's per-capita figures and names
  Beallsville. Beallsville is in adjacent Washington County, Pa., not in the coverage area.
  Quoted for the context Tetz gave, not as Belmont County reporting.
- **The national tour** — the "31 communities nationwide" figure comes from the Belmont County
  Tourism event page. Belmont News could not open the national program's own schedule at
  `thewallthatheals.org` to check it against a second source; see the access note below. No
  other state's stop is described anywhere in this story.

## Sourcing gap notes — printed, not hidden

1. **58,281 vs. 58,318.** Unresolved. Two pages we could open say 58,281; the Times Leader
   says 58,318; the county's own minutes round to "more than 58,000." The story names all of
   them and states no unqualified total.
2. **Attendance.** The only figure is "more than 8,000," Tetz's, as quoted by the Times
   Leader. Belmont News could not reach Tetz and found no attendance count published by the
   organizer, the tourism council or the county. The same Times Leader story describes the
   visit as covering "the past five days" while also dating the display Oct. 1-4; the
   organizer's site, the county tourism page and the county's own signed minutes all give
   Oct. 1-4. We print four days, sourced, and do not repeat the "five days" phrasing.
3. **Commissioner's remarks.** Sourced to the Times Leader only. The county's News & Events
   page has no events, `belmontcountycommissioners.com/the-commissioners` returns 404, and
   posted minutes stop at Sept. 16, 2026. Gianangeli's office is sourced to the county's own
   "Meet the Commissioners" page. We do not imply the county published anything about the
   appearance that it did not publish.
4. **National program pages.** `thewallthatheals.org` and `vvmf.org` are behind a Cloudflare
   human-verification interstitial and returned **HTTP 403 with a "Just a moment..." body** on
   a re-check at roughly 09:2x-09:3x EDT today, to a plain fetch, to a Chrome user-agent, and to a
   full Safari browser header set. They are not readable from this newsroom's runner. An
   earlier attempt in this same reporting day was recorded as reaching them on a browser
   user-agent retry; that result did not reproduce, so it is not relied on here. **Nothing in
   this story is sourced to either page.** The 58,281 figure and the "31 communities" line are
   sourced only to the organizer's site and the county tourism page. A reader with a working
   browser can open both national pages; if QA opens one and finds 58,281 there, that is a
   third source for a number this story already reports from two.
5. **No visitor count, no revenue figure, no per-hour count** is published anywhere Belmont
   News could reach. None is in this story.
